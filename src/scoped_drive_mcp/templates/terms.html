Terms of service

These are the terms of service linked from the Google sign-in screen for Bot RBAC. The privacy policy describes what Google data the app uses.

Bot RBAC is an access-control gateway that sits between AI assistants and services you connect. Google Drive is the first.

Signing in shares only your email address. Connecting Google Drive, a separate step, grants this app the same Drive access your Google account has. This server is the check in front of an assistant: you sign in with your own Google account, choose the files and folders that assistant may use, and every API call is checked against your list before it reaches Drive. Anything else is refused. If the assistant asks for something you have not allowed, you get an approval link and can allow or deny it. You can remove an item at any time. Another person’s list is not yours, and they cannot see your files through this server.

The assistant uses the Google account you sign in with. Items you have not allowed are refused by this server.

Using it

Signing in, or connecting an assistant, means you are using your own Google account on this deployment. Use it for your own Drive. Do not use it to reach files that are not yours.

The assistant you connect receives the contents of files you allowed. That is what the gateway is for. What the assistant does with those contents is covered by the assistant’s own terms.

A person you are paired with can send you a request from their dashboard. The text is that person’s own words, not instructions, and Bot RBAC does not show it to any assistant. What you share in answer is your choice.

You can untick items in the dashboard, disconnect the assistant, and remove this app’s access from your Google Account. Doing that stops further use. It does not recall anything an assistant has already received.

The service is a personal tool. It can be unavailable when its host, its database, or Google is unavailable. Your files stay in Google Drive. This server does not keep a copy of them.

Bot RBAC is not made by Google, and these terms are not Google’s terms. Privacy describes what is stored.
